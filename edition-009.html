CASE STUDY: Rentoza (Pty) Ltd — Voluntary Business Rescue, 1 July 2026

Rentoza is a Sandton-based subscription platform offering smartphones, laptops, appliances, baby products and fitness equipment on a rental basis without upfront ownership or a credit check. Founded in 2017 by Avinesh Reddy, Aviraag Ramdhani, Chris Govender and Mishaan Ratan, the company grew to approximately 14,000 active subscriptions and raised a cumulative $7.3m across seed and follow-on rounds from the Mineworkers Investment Company (Khulisani Ventures), Alitheia IDF, the Vumela Fund and Startupbootcamp AfriTech, including a Series A round in September 2023.

Per Rentoza's own board resolution (adopted 11 June 2026) and published business rescue documentation, the company's directors resolved to commence voluntary business rescue under Section 129 of the Companies Act, citing an inability to secure follow-on equity or debt funding, incomplete statutory audits for the 2024 and 2025 financial years, and liquidity pressure sufficient that the company disclosed it would be unable to pay creditors for approximately six months. CIPC data cited in media reporting shows Rentoza recorded turnover above R25m in each of 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, though profitability was not disclosed. The company has issued Section 129 and Section 147 notices to creditors and staff and opened a formal creditor claims window. It continues to trade in the ordinary course.

Mpoti April Moalusi CA(SA), CFA is the appointed business rescue practitioner, supported by Anthill Advisory, with Ernest van Zyl of EW van Zyl Attorneys as legal advisor. An initial meeting with creditors and staff is scheduled for 17 July 2026; a formal rescue plan — expected to involve balance-sheet restructuring, settlement negotiations with major creditors, and a search for a strategic partner or buyer — is targeted for 4 September 2026. Both dates fall beyond this edition's window and will be tracked in future editions.

Legal & Court Intelligence
One previously unreported, verified judgment was located this cycle via SAFLII. No judgment dated within the week ending 6 July 2026 itself was located.
Msunduzi Municipality v Capital City Housing NPC and Others
(823/2024) [2026] ZASCA 83 • Supreme Court of Appeal • 4 June 2026 • Coram: Matojane, Kgoele, Coppin JJA, Cloete and Opperman AJJA
VERIFIED

Facts & Holding: Capital City Housing NPC and its business rescue practitioner approached the KwaZulu-Natal High Court, Pietermaritzburg seeking a declaration that clause 1.32 of the Msunduzi Municipality's rates policy, and the definition of "specified public benefit activity" in section 1 of the Local Government: Municipal Property Rates Act, were constitutionally inconsistent — together with an order amending those provisions to include land and housing activities under Part I of the Ninth Schedule to the Income Tax Act, and retrospective reassessment of rates levied since 30 July 2019. The high court (Mngadi J) declined the relief sought. The SCA's judgment of 4 June 2026 addresses the appeal from that refusal.

BRP Impact: A reminder that a business rescue practitioner may pursue constitutional and administrative-law relief on a company's behalf where a rates or tax classification issue materially affects a rescue plan's viability — but that such applications, being novel constitutional challenges, face a high bar and are not a quick or reliable remedy for a company's rates burden during rescue.

Note on timing: This judgment predates the week ending 6 July 2026 but had not previously been reported in this publication; it is included here for completeness under this publication's ongoing SAFLII verification protocol rather than as a new-this-week development.

Contrarian Intelligence
One contrarian position this edition, anchored on this cycle's central observation: the absence of news.
01 — A Quiet Week on Johannesburg's Eskom Dispute Should Increase, Not Decrease, Reader Vigilance

Consensus: No news on the Eskom–Johannesburg dispute this week is mildly reassuring — the absence of an escalatory statement suggests the situation is stable ahead of the 24 July decision.

Contrarian view: A three-week silence between a 30-day PAJA extension and the decision date it produced is the least informative period in the entire dispute timeline — precisely because both a negotiated settlement and preparations for a phased interruption would look identical from the outside (no public statement) until the 24 July communication itself. Readers should not update their probability estimate of either outcome based on this week's silence.

Evidence: Across the dispute's timeline since 19 May 2026, every material update (payment figures, deadline extensions) has arrived in clusters tied to specific procedural checkpoints (notice issuance, deadline dates, ministerial intervention), not at a steady weekly cadence. This week fell between two such checkpoints.

Implication: BRPs and credit officers with Gauteng exposure should treat the 17 July submissions deadline and 24 July decision date as the next genuine information events, not assume the current quiet period reflects the eventual outcome in either direction.

Falsification: If Eskom or the City issues a substantive statement before 17 July confirming a negotiated resolution is imminent, this "uninformative silence" thesis would be wrong for this specific case.
