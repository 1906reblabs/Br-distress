Tongaat Hulett: RGS Group's Application to Set Aside the Rescue Plan Dismissed on Lis Alibi Pendens
The KwaZulu-Natal High Court (Durban), per Judge Rithy Singh, dismissed an application by RGS Group Holdings to set aside Tongaat Hulett Limited's adopted business rescue plan, in a judgment delivered Friday, 24 July 2026 VERIFIED. The court held that RGS's application duplicated an earlier application RGS lodged in November 2024 that remains pending before another court — the doctrine of lis alibi pendens (the same dispute already being litigated elsewhere) barred the fresh application from proceeding. This is a procedural, not a merits, ruling: the underlying November 2024 challenge to the plan continues, as does the R11.73bn disputed claim referenced in the RGS Group Holdings Ltd v Albertyn N.O. judgment reported in Edition 008. It is nonetheless the most significant verified THL development since Edition 008, and directionally supportive of the Vision Group's ability to continue implementing the plan without a second parallel legal front. [Source: The Witness, 27 July 2026]

RGS Group Holdings (Pty) Ltd v Tongaat Hulett Limited and Others — Application to Set Aside Adopted Rescue Plan Dismissed
KwaZulu-Natal High Court, Durban • Judgment delivered Friday, 24 July 2026 • Presiding: Singh J
VERIFIED

Facts & Holding: RGS Group Holdings applied to have Tongaat Hulett Limited's adopted business rescue plan set aside. The court dismissed the application on the basis of lis alibi pendens — the same substantive dispute is already the subject of an application RGS lodged in November 2024 that remains pending elsewhere. The court did not rule on the merits of RGS's underlying challenge to the plan.

BRP Impact: Confirms that a dissenting creditor cannot obtain a second bite at the same substantive challenge via a freshly framed application while an earlier, materially identical application remains outstanding. BRPs facing repeat or duplicative litigation from a single dissenting creditor should consider raising lis pendens at the earliest opportunity.

Market Impact: Removes one procedural front in the long-running THL/RGS dispute without resolving the R11.73bn claim at its centre. The Vision Group's implementation of the adopted plan can proceed without this parallel challenge, but the substantive November 2024 application, and its associated contingent liability, remain open. See Edition 011's 180-Day Forecast on THL's rescue exit timeline (Section 13/14).

Case citation pending SAFLII indexing; reported via The Witness, 27 July 2026, citing the judgment directly. This publication will confirm the formal neutral citation once indexed.

Brand v Morgan Creek Boerdery (Pty) Ltd and Others — Standing of a Contingent Creditor to Apply for Business Rescue
[2025] ZAWCHC 421 • Western Cape High Court • Decided 11 September 2025 • Reported in South African Law Reports, July 2026 issue
VERIFIED

Facts & Holding: The court considered whether an applicant whose claim against the respondent company was contingent rather than actual and liquidated had standing under Section 131(1) of the Companies Act to apply for the company to be placed under business rescue. The judgment, decided in September 2025, was brought to broader practitioner attention through the South African Law Reports' business-rescue commentary column published in July 2026.

BRP / Creditor Impact: Relevant to creditors assessing whether disputed or not-yet-crystallised claims are sufficient to found a rescue application, and to respondent companies assessing whether an applicant creditor's standing can be challenged as a threshold defence.

Note on timing: This judgment predates this edition's window but had not previously been reported in this publication; it is included here for completeness under this publication's standing legal-verification protocol, consistent with how the Msunduzi Municipality judgment was reported in Edition 009.

01 — The SAPO Correction Suggests SAPO's Rescue Is a Stronger Precedent for Government-Backed Rescue Than This Publication Previously Credited It For

Consensus (implicit in this publication's own prior framing): SAPO's rescue was a large, ongoing, uncertain government bailout — an open-ended R890m facility supporting a 36-month transformation involving roughly 10,000 job losses, with the outcome still very much in doubt.

Contrarian view: The corrected record shows something closer to a completed, disciplined restructuring: a 2023 government allocation of R2.4bn, an already-executed and closed compromise (12c/R1, paid to 99%+ of creditors by August 2024), a already-completed retrenchment programme (4,342 employees, finished April 2024), a materially improved balance sheet (from −R7.9bn to +R840m net asset value), and a BRP-initiated termination application already before the court on the basis of substantial implementation. This is not an open-ended bailout in progress; on the verified record, it reads as a rescue that has already done most of its restructuring work and is now in a wind-down-to-termination phase, with one unresolved financing question (the R3.8bn digital tranche) rather than an unresolved rescue.

Evidence: The BRPs' own termination application, lodged 12 June 2026 on substantial-implementation grounds, is itself the strongest evidence that the practitioners regard the core restructuring as complete rather than ongoing.

Implication: Readers who have discounted SAPO as a stalled or perpetually-uncertain SOE rescue should revisit that view. The open question is narrower than previously framed: whether R3.8bn in additional digital-modernisation funding can be secured, not whether the underlying restructuring succeeded.

Falsification: If the Gauteng High Court declines to terminate the rescue, or if SAPO's FY2027 results show the improved balance sheet reversing, this view is wrong.

02 — A Publication That Finds Its Own Errors Months Later Should Disclose That Lag Honestly, Not Just the Correction

Consensus (implicit in this publication's own self-audit framing): Disclosing a correction in full — original claim, correct figure, source — meets this publication's editorial standard for honesty.

Contrarian view: Disclosure of the correction itself is necessary but not sufficient. That is a different and less reassuring finding than "we caught an error quickly" — it is closer to "some figures in this publication's own back catalogue may be stale for months before anyone checks."

Evidence: Section 14's corrections register below states the precise gap for each item: the SAPO figure went unverified across roughly nine editions of continuous reference; the Q1 liquidation figure was already superseded at first publication.

Implication: This publication should not treat a repeated, uncorrected figure as more reliable merely because it has been repeated. Falsification: If a review of this publication's next ten editions finds no further multi-edition-old figure requiring correction, this concern would be overstated in hindsight.

CORRECTION #1 — SAPO Government Funding Figure (Carried Since Editions 004–005, Repeated Through Edition 011)

What this publication has repeated: "R890m in emergency operational funding" approved as Section 135(3)(a) post-commencement government funding (first reported Edition 004), described as providing "14-month operational runway" (Edition 005), and referenced again in Edition 011 as "the most recent verified financing position located" without independent re-checking.

Correction issued: The R890m figure is retired. Section 06, Section 08, and Section 09 of this edition carry the corrected financing and retrenchment record in full.
