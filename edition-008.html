Section 01
Executive Intelligence Summary
The week ending 29 June 2026 is the most positive week for South African business rescue outcomes since this publication began tracking: two of the country's largest and longest-running rescue processes reached successful conclusions within 48 hours of each other, while a third avoided liquidation entirely. Murray & Roberts Limited closed its R1.27bn Differential Capital mining transaction on 25 June, and Group Five formally terminated six years of business rescue on 30 June with all creditors paid. These verified outcomes, combined with a sharp fuel price relief from 1 July, are offset by one unresolved risk carried over from Edition 007: Eskom extended, rather than resolved, its Johannesburg bulk-supply dispute, pushing the decision date from 24 June to 24 July. SADI falls three points to 55/100 — the lowest reading in this publication's eight-edition history.
1
Murray & Roberts Closes R1.27bn Mining Sale to Differential Capital — 2,600 Jobs Secured
Murray & Roberts Limited (MRL), in business rescue since November 2024, completed the sale of its Cementation mining services businesses (Africa and the Americas) and Terra Nova Technologies to an investor consortium led by Differential Capital on 25 June 2026 VERIFIED. The R1.27bn transaction — structured as R1bn payable at closing and a R270m deferred payment due 12 months later — allowed the joint business rescue practitioners (Josh Cunliffe of Metis Strategic Advisors, among others) to settle all secured lender debt and post-commencement finance obligations in full. The adopted rescue plan received 100% creditor approval in April 2026. Unsecured creditors will not receive a distribution until the deferred R270m tranche is received. The MRL business rescue process itself remains open pending that final settlement. Japie du Plessis becomes CEO of the new standalone mining services platform; Sibulele Songca is CFO. [Source: Business Day, Mining Weekly, News24, IOL Business Report, Werksmans Attorneys client alert, all 29 June–22 July 2026]

Group Five Formally Exits Business Rescue After Six Years — All Creditors Paid in Full
Group Five Construction (Pty) Ltd and Group Five Limited terminated business rescue proceedings on 30 June 2026, practitioners Dave Lake and Peter van den Steen of Metis Strategic Advisors confirmed VERIFIED. Group Five entered business rescue in March 2019 holding approximately R7bn in creditor and contingent exposures across more than 2,300 creditors, 119 active projects, and close to 6,000 employees in 180 companies across 38 countries — at the time, an independent PwC assessment found secured creditors would have recovered only around 65% in immediate liquidation. Under the executed plans, all secured and preferential creditors have been paid in full, concurrent creditors have been paid or had funds set aside in accordance with the plan, and the practitioners say a surplus return to shareholders is now possible — an outcome Lake described as uncommon "in complex business rescue processes of this scale." The group now enters a finalisation phase (asset sales, residual litigation, audit and tax matters) with 54 of the original 179 group companies remaining. [Source: Engineering News, Business Day, IOL Business Report, News24, The Paper, 30 June–1 July 2026; G5.co.za business rescue status filings]

Fuel Prices Fall Sharply From 1 July Despite Full Levy Reinstatement — Brent Crashes on Iran-US MOU

Legal & Court Intelligence
Three verified judgments this cycle, all confirmed on SAFLII. All case names, citations, courts, dates, and holdings below were checked directly against the SAFLII record.
Great Lakes Consultancy (Pty) Ltd v Schoonspruit Development (Pty) Ltd and Others
(2025/131798) [2026] ZAKZPHC 70 • KwaZulu-Natal High Court, Pietermaritzburg • 18 June 2026
VERIFIED

Facts & Holding: The court placed Schoonspruit Development (Pty) Ltd and Agri Industria (Pty) Ltd under supervision and commenced business rescue proceedings under s131(1), appointing Dean Du Toit and Kurt Robert Knoop as business rescue practitioners. The judgment separately records that a Western Cape High Court provisional liquidation order had already been granted against Schoonspruit on 6 August 2025 notwithstanding the pending rescue application — confirming, and applying on its facts, the position in Blue Star Holdings (Pty) Ltd v West Coast Oyster Growers CC 2013 (6) SA 540 (WCC) that a business rescue application does not itself bar a court from granting a provisional winding-up order.

BRP Impact: Practitioners should not assume that filing or supporting a business rescue application automatically halts a parallel liquidation application in a different division; the two processes can run concurrently until one court adjudicates.

Zizwe Open Cast Mining (Pty) Ltd v Lethabo Minerals (Pty) Ltd and Another
(2026/104756) [2026] ZANWHC 161 • North West High Court • 15 June 2026
VERIFIED

Facts & Holding: Following a formal demand issued 28 April 2026, the applicant sought a business rescue order after the respondent failed to cease trading, voluntarily commence rescue, or otherwise respond. The judgment restates the "reasonable prospects of rescue" standard as more than an arguable possibility but less than a reasonable probability, applying Oakdene Square Properties (Pty) Ltd v Farm Bothasfontein (Kyalami) (Pty) Ltd [2013] ZASCA 68 as the foundational authority and Newcity Group (Pty) Ltd v Pellow NO [2014] ZASCA 162 as confirming that a court exercises a wide-sense value judgment on the primary facts — the court held neither authority displaces the other. On jurisdiction, the court held that jurisdiction is determined by the respondent company's registered address, and that a non-exclusive jurisdiction clause in a commercial agreement does not prevent an applicant from approaching the court of competent jurisdiction.

BRP / Creditor Impact: A useful, current restatement of the Oakdene/Newcity standard for practitioners assessing whether an application meets the rescue threshold, and a reminder that registered-office jurisdiction generally governs notwithstanding contractual jurisdiction clauses.

RGS Group Holdings Ltd v Albertyn N.O. and Others (re: Tongaat Hulett Limited business rescue)
(2026-031780) [2026] ZAKZDHC 38 • KwaZulu-Natal High Court, Durban • 24 July 2026
VERIFIED

Facts & Holding: This judgment records that at the 17 June 2026 hearing, Tongaat Hulett Limited's (THL) business rescue practitioners were granted leave to withdraw a liquidation application after post-commencement finance from the IDC was extended to September 2026 to enable the Vision Group to implement the business rescue plan adopted by creditors on 11 January 2024 — directly corroborating the verified media reporting in Section 01, Item 3. Separately, the court dismissed applicant RGS Group Holdings' counter-application seeking urgent execution relief, finding RGS would suffer no irreparable harm without it, notwithstanding RGS's contention that the Vision Group's demand for payment of an R11.73bn claim against THL during the currency of business rescue was an abuse of process intended to hold THL hostage and block future rescue plans.

Market Impact: Confirms from an independent court record — rather than media reporting alone — that the Tongaat Hulett liquidation withdrawal and IDC PCF extension reported this edition are accurate. The R11.73bn disputed claim by the Vision Group against THL remains a live, material contingent liability within the rescue process and should be treated as an open risk factor by any party assessing THL's rescue plan.

“A business rescue application does not, by itself, halt a parallel liquidation application in another division — the two processes can run concurrently until a court adjudicates one of them.”
— Great Lakes Consultancy v Schoonspruit Development, applying Blue Star Holdings v West Coast Oyster Growers CC

Section 12
Contrarian Intelligence
Two contrarian positions this edition, each anchored in this cycle's verified evidence.
01 — Group Five and Murray & Roberts Are Not Evidence the Rescue Mechanism "Works" — They Are Survivorship Evidence From the Tiny Minority of Cases Large Enough to Attract Institutional Capital

Consensus: Two major, long-running South African business rescues concluding successfully in the same week is a positive signal for the rescue mechanism generally — proof that Chapter 6 can deliver real outcomes for creditors even in large, complex cases.

Contrarian view: Both successful outcomes required an ingredient unavailable to the overwhelming majority of South African rescue cases: a large, well-capitalised acquirer or DFI (Differential Capital; the IDC) willing to write a nine- or ten-figure cheque. Group Five's six-year process and Murray & Roberts' 100%-approved plan are evidence that the mechanism works well for R1bn+, institutionally-followed distress situations — not evidence about the typical rescue case, which this publication's own prior editions (before the Verified-Only policy) repeatedly characterised as small, government-contract-dependent, and PCF-starved. Verified Stats SA data this edition shows Q1 2026 company liquidations up 15% year-on-year even as these two large cases concluded well.

Evidence: Stats SA: 377 Q1 2026 liquidations (companies + CCs), up 15% versus March 2025; June 2026 liquidations flat at 245 versus 246 a year earlier — i.e., the aggregate system shows no material improvement in the same window that produced two flagship successes. IDC and Differential Capital, the capital providers behind this week's two verified wins, are both large institutional players; neither is a template replicable at the scale of a typical mid-market rescue.

Implication: Readers should not extrapolate from Murray & Roberts and Group Five to a broader claim that rescue outcomes are improving system-wide. The two data points are genuinely positive and genuinely verified — but they describe the top of the distribution, not its middle.

Falsification: If Stats SA's Q3 2026 liquidation count shows a material year-on-year decline (rather than the flat-to-rising pattern seen in Q1 and June 2026), this view is wrong and a broader system improvement is underway.

02 — The Eskom-Johannesburg Extension Is a Rational Deferral, Not a Retreat — and BRPs Should Read the 30-Day Extension as a Signal the Underlying Number Is Moving in the City's Favour

Consensus: Eskom's second extension of the Johannesburg PAJA process (per OUTA's own framing) simply delays an inevitable reckoning — "the crisis has been delayed, not resolved."

Contrarian view: The verified payment data cuts against the "delay only" framing. Johannesburg/City Power paid approximately R1.2bn to Eskom in the roughly five weeks between the notice being issued and 11 June 2026 — a genuine, verifiable paydown rate that, if sustained, materially changes the arrears trajectory well before any 24 July decision. A regulator extending a public-participation process after the regulated party begins paying at scale is consistent with a negotiated de-escalation in progress, not merely bureaucratic delay.

Evidence: R1.2bn paid against a base of R5.28bn (roughly 23% of the total exposure) inside five weeks is a materially faster paydown rate than this publication has verified for any other Eskom-municipal dispute tracked in prior editions (Tshwane's R1.93bn reduction, by contrast, took 14 months).

Implication: BRPs with Gauteng-concentrated rescue mandates should not default to worst-case planning around 24 July. The verified payment trend, if it continues at a similar pace through mid-July, would put the City within a plausible range of a negotiated settlement rather than an interruption.

Falsification: If Eskom's own July disclosures show the R1.2bn paydown was a one-off catch-up payment rather than a sustained rate, and City of Johannesburg arrears have not meaningfully reduced further by the 17 July submissions deadline, this view is wrong.
