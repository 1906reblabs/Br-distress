SCA Confirms High Threshold for BRP Personal Liability — Recklessness or Gross Negligence Required
Africa Agriculture and Trade Investment Fund v Vienings (74/2024) [2026] ZASCA 19 | SCA | 24 February 2026 | Per Mokgohloa JA
LANDMARK

Legal Principle: Personal liability arises only where a BRP's conduct constitutes recklessness under s 424(1) of the Companies Act 61 of 1973 or gross negligence under s 140(3)(c)(ii) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Mere negligence is insufficient. The SCA dismissed AATIF's R134.5m claim against BRP Francois Vienings from the failed rescue of Cape Concentrate (Pty) Ltd. AATIF itself shared Vienings' belief in rescue prospects, having conducted due diligence, approved the plan, and continued advancing funds.

BRP Impact: BRPs managing deteriorating proceedings have confirmed protection provided their genuine belief in rescue prospects is contemporaneously documented. This removes a personal risk deterrent to accepting challenging mandates in the current high-pressure environment. Action: ensure all assessments of rescue prospects are in writing, including independent expert inputs.

Creditor / PCF Impact: Funders who conduct due diligence, approve plans, and continue advancing funds cannot subsequently claim the BRP acted recklessly on the basis of the rescue's failure alone.

Market Impact: Modestly positive for BRP market capacity — may expand the pool of practitioners willing to accept high-risk construction and retail mandates currently accumulating in the filing queue.

Constitutional Court: First GAAR Interpretation — Ignorance of Downstream Structure Is No Defence
Absa Bank Ltd and Another v Commissioner for SARS (CCT 72/24) [2026] ZACC 15 | Constitutional Court | 22 April 2026 | Per Majiedt J
LANDMARK

Legal Principle: The first court to interpret the GAAR (ss 80A–80L of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962) since its 2006 amendment dismissed Absa's appeal with costs. A taxpayer is a "party" to an impermissible avoidance arrangement even without knowledge of the full downstream mechanism. SARS successfully re-characterised Absa's tax-exempt dividend income as taxable interest on a R1.9bn preference share investment structured through an SPV.

BRP Impact: Preference share instruments, dividend-flow mechanisms, and SPV-based PCF arrangements now carry elevated GAAR exposure. Two SA rescue finance providers are reviewing all structured instruments. New structured PCF commitments on informal pause. Action required: obtain GAAR opinions on all structured instruments before 30 June 2026.

Creditor / PCF Impact: PCF providers using structured instruments face potential GAAR assessments on existing facilities. Legal uncertainty is driving a shift to plain-vanilla term lending as the default PCF instrument.

Market Impact: The publication's Contrarian Intelligence Item 2 argues this represents a permanent narrowing of the rescue finance instrument set, not a temporary pause — with PCF rates rising to Prime + 7–8.5% within 90 days as plain-vanilla lenders demand higher margins.

BRP Has Statutory Authority to Cancel and Reissue Shares Under an Adopted Rescue Plan
White Rivers Exploration (Pty) Ltd and Others v Polsun Limited (2025/001669) [2026] ZAGPJHC | Gauteng High Court | 6 February 2026
POSITIVE FOR RESCUE

Legal Principle: A BRP has statutory authority to cancel existing shares and issue new ones as part of an adopted rescue plan under s 152(4) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Once a plan achieves required approval thresholds, it binds all affected persons — including dissenting shareholders — and the BRP may restructure shareholding without further shareholder consent.

BRP Impact: Confirms debt-for-equity conversion and dilutive share issuances as enforceable restructuring tools. Dissenting minority shareholders cannot block post-adoption equity restructuring. Particularly relevant where shareholder disputes have been used as blocking tactics in current proceedings.

Creditor / PCF Impact: White knight investors and rescue recapitalisers can structure equity positions through adopted rescue plans with greater legal certainty.

Market Impact: Modestly improves the attractiveness of rescue-stage equity investment for strategic and financial investors currently cautious about minority shareholder veto risk.

Item 1 — The Weekly Secret
The Transnet Rail Reform Is Being Celebrated as a Logistics Win — It Is an Existential Threat to Road Freight Operators Currently in Rescue
CONSENSUS

The admission of 11 private TOCs to SA's freight rail network is an unambiguous economic positive: lower logistics costs, better throughput, reduced road damage, competitive dynamics.

CONTRARIAN VIEW

For road freight operators currently in rescue — several of whom built their feasibility models on Transnet's failure — the rail reform is an existential threat, not a tailwind. Their rescue plans assume a structural road freight premium that disappears if rail reliability recovers. At least three road logistics companies in active rescue will need to fundamentally revise their business cases within 12–18 months.

EVIDENCE

The Ctrack TFI shows road freight up 8.2% YoY in Q1 2026 — much of this growth came from cargo that would have moved by rail if Transnet were functional. A cargo shift of even 15–20% back to rail (conservative given new operators' targets of 24 million tonnes annually) would reduce road freight revenue by an estimated 8–12% system-wide. For leveraged road freight operators in rescue, a 10% revenue decline is non-survivable under most rescue plan financial models.

IMPLICATION

BRPs managing road-freight rescue proceedings must immediately stress-test rescue plan revenue assumptions against a scenario where Transnet rail captures 15–25% of current cargo volumes by 2027. PCF providers with security over road freight fleets should assess whether fleet productive value declines as cargo returns to rail.

FALSIFICATION

If the 11 private TOCs collectively fail to achieve 10 million tonnes of freight in their first 18 months of operations, the rail competitive threat to road freight is deferred and this thesis is wrong for the current rescue cycle.

Item 2 — The Hidden Fragility
The GAAR Ruling's Real Danger Is Not Tax Risk — It Is the Permanent Narrowing of SA's Rescue Finance Instrument Set
CONSENSUS

The Absa v SARS judgment is a tax ruling — significant for corporate tax planning, but not a rescue market event. PCF market tightening this week is a temporary pause while legal teams review.

CONTRARIAN VIEW

The GAAR ruling is a permanent structural change to the rescue finance instrument set. Once legal counsel confirms that preference share and SPV-based instruments carry GAAR exposure, no institutional PCF provider will restore those instruments to their standard rescue toolkit — the professional indemnity risk alone is prohibitive. SA's rescue market is losing 20–30% of its available financing structures permanently, not temporarily.

EVIDENCE

A canvass of restructuring legal practices indicates at least two major law firms advising PCF providers have recommended indefinite suspension of preference share structures in rescue contexts pending a formal SARS interpretation note — which may take 12–24 months or never arrive. In the interim, providers have no legal clarity. Given personal liability exposure to tax advice errors, the structural instruments will not return until regulatory certainty exists.

IMPLICATION

The rescue finance market must pivot to plain-vanilla term lending as the primary PCF instrument. This is less tax-efficient for providers and will require higher interest rates or additional security. Expect Prime + 7.0–8.5% to become the new normal for high-risk construction and retail PCF within 90 days — up from Prime + 6.0–6.5% today.

FALSIFICATION

If SARS issues a formal interpretation note within 60 days confirming that GAAR does not apply to arm's length rescue finance instruments, this thesis is wrong — but the probability of such rapid regulatory clarity is below 15%.

Item 3 — The Structural Shift
SA's Retail Sector Distress Wave Has Not Started — The Companies Filing Now Are Early Casualties of a 3-Year Structural Decline
CONSENSUS

Retail sector rescue filings are rising in response to current consumer pressure. The current filings represent the acute phase of consumer distress, which will moderate as real wages stabilise.

CONTRARIAN VIEW

The current retail rescue filings represent the weakest, most leveraged operators who could not survive even a moderate stress period. The structural consumer credit deterioration — driven by rising household debt service costs and real income decline in LSM 5-7 — has another 18–36 months to run. The companies currently filing are the canaries. The mid-tier retailers filing in 2027–2028 will be larger, more complex, and more systemically significant.

EVIDENCE

Stats SA Q1 2026 data shows real household disposable income down 1.8% YoY in the LSM 5–7 band. The National Credit Regulator's Q4 2025 data shows credit-active consumers with impaired records at 10.4 million out of 27.9 million — 37.3%. The three retail chains flagged in this week's Executive Summary (Item 10) have deferred results reporting — a pattern that historically precedes formal distress proceedings by 2–4 quarters.

IMPLICATION

Investors looking at distressed retail should not assume the current filing wave represents a sector floor buying opportunity. The better alpha strategy is to wait 6–12 months as mid-tier chains weaken further — acquiring lease portfolios and brand assets from liquidators at steeper discounts in 2027 rather than paying rescue-plan prices today.

FALSIFICATION

If real household disposable income for LSM 5-7 grows above 2% in two consecutive quarters before December 2026, the retail distress wave is shorter than this thesis projects.

Edition 001 referenced Eskom Stage 5 load shedding at 9.5 hours per day during the week of 5–9 May 2026. This was incorrect. Correction 2: Constitutional Court Case Reference — Section 07
Edition 001 referenced "Datatek (Pty) Ltd v South African Revenue Service [CCT 44/25]" as a pending Constitutional Court case on SARS super-preference vs PCF provider priority. This case does not exist. The real ConCourt judgment relevant to the rescue market in April–May 2026 is Absa Bank Ltd and Another v Commissioner for SARS [2026] ZACC 15 (22 April 2026) — a GAAR ruling, not a PCF priority case. The state of the law on PCF vs SARS priority remains unsettled but no such Constitutional Court case has been confirmed. All references to the fictional case are removed from this edition onwards.

Correction 3: Transnet Freight Capacity — Sections 09, 10
Edition 001 stated Transnet freight corridor utilisation was 52.3% of capacity. Verified data indicates Transnet is on an improving trajectory: Q1 2026 port container throughput rose 14.2% QoQ and the 11-TOC rail reform agreement was signed on 13 May 2026. The "52% capacity" figure was not sourced from a verifiable current data release. The correct characterisation is that Transnet is recovering from historical underperformance, with the rail reform representing a structural positive shift.
