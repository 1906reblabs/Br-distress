Neethling v Louis Group (SA) (Pty) Ltd and Others
(8249/2024) [2026] ZAWCHC 245 • Western Cape High Court • 18 May 2026 • Rule nisi returnable 20 July 2026
VERIFIED

Facts & Holding: Business rescue proceedings in respect of Louis Group (SA) (Pty) Ltd had commenced by resolution dated 17 May 2023. Creditors rejected the proposed business rescue plan at a meeting on 5 April 2024; an application to set aside that creditor vote was dismissed. The court joined five further respondents, dismissed a striking-out application, and ordered that the business rescue proceedings be converted into liquidation proceedings, placing Louis Group (SA) (Pty) Ltd under provisional liquidation in the hands of the Master of the High Court. A rule nisi was issued, returnable 20 July 2026, calling on interested parties to show cause why a final liquidation order should not be granted. The judgment engages directly with the two-stage versus single-value-judgment debate on interpreting section 153(7) of the Companies Act (as considered in FirstRand and Ferrostaal), holding that creditors' subjective motivations at a plan-rejection vote are not relevant considerations under the court's section 153(7)/153(1)(a)(ii) analysis.

BRP / Creditor Impact: Confirms that a rejected rescue plan, once unsuccessfully challenged, converts cleanly to liquidation on the ordinary section 153 pathway, and that the court's inquiry on such a conversion is confined to objective statutory factors rather than creditors' stated reasons for rejecting a plan. Practitioners contesting a plan-rejection vote should not expect a court to weigh the subjective motives of dissenting creditors.

Market Impact: The rule nisi return date of 20 July 2026 is a genuine, dated, verifiable event for any party tracking the disposition of Louis Group (SA)'s assets; see Section 10 for the associated analytical note.

Contrarian Intelligence
One contrarian position this edition, anchored on the genuinely new item this cycle: the SARB economist split.
01 — A Single Dissenting Bank Forecast Is Being Read as "Market Uncertainty" When It May Simply Be One Bank's House View

Consensus: The emergence of a bank forecasting a hike against a broader hold consensus ahead of 23 July signals genuine, elevated uncertainty in the SARB decision — a meaningfully more contested setup than the "stand pat through year-end" consensus reported since Edition 007.

Contrarian view: A single dissenting institutional forecast is not the same as a divided consensus. FocusEconomics' own panel, cited in this publication since Edition 007, has consistently shown a dispersion of views around the SARB's trajectory (including some panelists projecting further hikes and others projecting a resumption of cuts) without that dispersion previously being read as "the market is split." The 13 July report elevates one bank's house view to a narrative of contested consensus partly because it is the first dissent reported since the actual May hike, not because the underlying probability distribution has genuinely widened.

Evidence: The same 13 July reporting states that "most economists expect rates to remain unchanged" and frames the Nedbank call as the exception, not the rule. June CPI at 5.0% is a two-year high but remains within the SARB's target band upper bound, and the SARB's own quarterly projection model (last cited by this publication in Edition 006/007) had been pointing toward eventual easing, not further tightening.

Implication: BRPs and PCF providers should weight the hold scenario more heavily than this week's headline framing might suggest, while still stress-testing the hike scenario given the genuine two-year-high CPI print.

Falsification: If two or more additional major banks publish hike forecasts before 23 July, the "single dissenting view" framing is wrong and a genuinely divided consensus has emerged.
